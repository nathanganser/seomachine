# Magic Heidi Brand Voice & Messaging

This document defines the Magic Heidi brand voice, tone, and messaging framework. Reference this when writing all content to ensure consistency.

## Brand Voice Pillars

### 1. Professional Yet Personal
- **What it means**: We're Swiss freelancers ourselves. We know invoicing, VAT, bookkeeping, expenses, and what is required to prepare tax returns as Swiss entrepreneurs, but we're not arrogant or difficult to approach. The CEO's phone number and email are on the website. We're experts, but we're not stuffy or overly formal.
- **How it sounds**: Conversational, warm, knowledgeable without being condescending. Like talking to a really knowledgeable, experienced friend on a topic.
- **Example**: "We know how confusing VAT registration can feel because we did it ourselves. Here are three things we learned that might save you some headaches."
- **Avoid**: Overly technical jargon without explanation, corporate-speak, talking down to readers, vague and fluffy language that doesn't convey any real message. Never be boring.

### 2. Fair, Honest and Truthful
- **What it means**: When comparing competitors, we follow Swiss law and ethics. No false claims, no exaggerations, and no unverifiable claims.
- **How it sounds**: Honest, clear, comparative, and objective.
- **Example**: "The amount of clicks required to get from our homepage to a generated invoice is 33, compared to 57 for our competitor."
- **Avoid**: Describing competitors as "inferior" or "clunky", or setting their "difficulty of use" to High and ours to Low without objective evidence.

### 3. Action Oriented
- **What it means**: We give clear tips, simple todos, links, checklists, and examples. No generic information that people cannot act on.
- **How it sounds**: Practical, direct, experienced, and specific. We tell people what to do next, what to watch out for, and where the common traps are.
- **Example**: "To become a freelancer in Switzerland, the first step isn't actually to register anywhere but just to start and issue invoices. Here's what you need to know..."
- **Avoid**: Explaining generic things like "First step is to choose your company name" when that is not actually the most useful next step for a Swiss freelancer.

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Vary length**: Mix short, direct sentences with slightly longer explanatory ones. Use short sentences for key warnings and next steps.
- **Active voice preferred**: "Magic Heidi helps you prepare your invoices" not "Invoices are helped by Magic Heidi."
- **Average length**: 12-20 words per sentence. Longer sentences are fine when explaining rules, but break them up quickly.
- **Clarity first**: If a freelancer would need to read a sentence twice, rewrite it. If a claim depends on canton, income level, VAT status, or business type, say so.

### Paragraph Structure
- **Length**: 2-4 sentences typically. Use one-sentence paragraphs for important takeaways or warnings.
- **One idea per paragraph**: Keep each paragraph focused on one decision, rule, example, or action.
- **Transitions**: Move from context to action. Explain why something matters, then what to do next.
- **White space**: Break up dense tax, VAT, and bookkeeping explanations with subheadings, bullets, examples, and checklists.

### Word Choice
- **Conversational**: Write how an experienced freelancer would explain something to another freelancer over coffee.
- **Specific**: "Keep the receipt and note the client/project" not "maintain proper documentation."
- **Action verbs**: "Create", "check", "send", "save", "register", "prepare", "compare", "update", "download".
- **Plain but precise**: Use correct Swiss administrative terms, then explain them in simple words.
- **Avoid fluff**: Cut "seamless", "revolutionary", "world-class", "empower", "unlock", and similar generic SaaS language unless it is immediately backed by a concrete benefit.

### Terminology

**Say This** → **Not That**
- Swiss freelancer / self-employed person → Entrepreneurial rockstar (Clear, respectful, not hype-driven)
- Sole proprietorship / sole trader → Company by default (Many freelancers do not need a separate company structure)
- Invoice → Bill / payment request (Invoice is clearer and more professional)
- VAT / TVA / MWST / IVA → Sales tax (Use Swiss terminology; explain when needed)
- Tax return preparation → Tax optimization magic (Be honest and avoid exaggerated tax promises)
- Receipt / expense record → Paperwork junk (Respect the importance of records, even when admin is annoying)
- Canton-specific rules → Universal Swiss rule (Avoid pretending every canton handles everything the same way)
- Tax professional / fiduciary → Tax wizard (Use professional language when advising escalation)

## Content Formatting

### Headlines
- **Be specific**: "How to Create Your First Swiss Freelance Invoice" not "Invoicing Tips".
- **Name the audience or situation**: Use "for Swiss freelancers", "as a sole proprietor", or "before VAT registration" when useful.
- **Include the practical outcome**: Tell readers what they will be able to do after reading.
- **Keep concise**: Aim for 50-65 characters for SEO when possible, but do not sacrifice clarity.

### Subheadings
- **Make them scannable**: A reader should understand the article flow from the subheadings alone.
- **Use questions freelancers actually ask**: "Do I need to register first?" often works better than "Registration considerations".
- **Put the action in the heading**: "Save these documents" is stronger than "Documentation".
- **Keep structure consistent**: If one section starts with a verb, use verbs for similar sections.

### Lists
- **Use numbered lists for steps**: Registration flows, invoice creation, tax-prep checklists, and setup processes should be numbered.
- **Use bullets for options or reminders**: Deductible expense examples, document lists, and common mistakes work well as bullets.
- **Keep items parallel**: Start each item with the same type of phrase when possible.
- **Make every item useful**: Avoid list filler. Each point should help the freelancer decide, check, or do something.

### Calls-to-Action
- **Lead with the next useful action**: "Create your first Swiss invoice" not "Get started" when the action is specific.
- **Match the reader's stage**: A beginner may need a checklist; an experienced freelancer may need a tool or comparison.
- **Be honest about the benefit**: Promise cleaner invoices, better records, or easier preparation, not guaranteed tax savings.
- **Keep it human**: CTAs can sound like help, not pressure: "Send us your question" or "Try it on your next invoice".

## Audience Understanding

### Who We Write For

**Primary Audience**: Swiss Freelancers and Self-Employed Professionals
- Work independently for clients in Switzerland or from Switzerland.
- Need to issue invoices, track expenses, prepare tax information, and understand obligations.
- May be new to freelancing or experienced but tired of messy admin.
- Often work across languages, cantons, currencies, or client types.
- Want practical answers, not accounting lectures or vague startup advice.

**Secondary Audiences**:
- **New Freelancers**: People sending their first invoices and trying to understand what they must do now versus later.
- **Side-Hustlers**: Employees or students earning freelance income who need simple guidance before things become complicated.
- **VAT-Ready Freelancers**: Freelancers approaching or crossing the VAT threshold who need to understand what changes.
- **International Freelancers in Switzerland**: People who understand their craft but not yet the Swiss administrative system.
- **Fiduciaries and Advisors**: Professionals who may recommend tools that help clients keep cleaner records.

### What They Care About

**Top Priorities**:
1. Sending correct, professional invoices.
2. Knowing what to keep for taxes and deductions.
3. Avoiding surprises with AHV/AVS, VAT, and tax deadlines.
4. Spending less time on admin and more time on paid client work.
5. Understanding Swiss-specific rules without needing to become an accountant.

**Pain Points**:
1. Not knowing which advice applies to Switzerland, their canton, or their business type.
2. Feeling unsure about when to register, what to declare, and what documents to keep.
3. Losing receipts or reconstructing expenses months later.
4. Finding VAT, AHV/AVS, and tax language intimidating or inconsistent across languages.
5. Worrying that a small admin mistake will become expensive or embarrassing.

### How to Serve Them

- **Respect their time**: Get to the practical point quickly and avoid long theoretical introductions.
- **Make it actionable**: Give checklists, examples, links, templates, and clear next steps.
- **Explain the Swiss context**: Say when rules depend on canton, revenue, VAT status, or business structure.
- **Be honest about limits**: Tell readers when general guidance is enough and when they should ask a fiduciary, tax office, or legal professional.
- **Reduce anxiety**: Make admin feel manageable without pretending it is always simple.
- **Stay human**: Write like someone who has also searched for the right tax form at 11 p.m.
