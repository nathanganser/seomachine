SEO Audit · Magic Heidi

Atlas: 250,220 pages, 94 clicks atlas.magicheidi.ch

Audited 21 Aug 2026 Window 22 Jul – 18 Aug 2026 Sources GSC · DataForSEO · direct crawl

Atlas is a well-built machine pointed at a market that doesn't exist, in languages nobody searches in, competing against Google using Google's own data.

The engineering is genuinely good — server-rendered HTML, correct canonicals, complete hreflang, valid LocalBusiness and ItemList structured data, 80–150 ms TTFB, clean robots.txt. None of that is the problem.

The problem is that 250,220 URLs were published against Romandie-only data, translated into three languages with no corresponding demand, and aimed at queries where Google answers with its own local pack. Twenty-eight days after launch, that has returned 94 clicks.

03

Six root causes

Ordered by severity. The first two are strategic and cannot be fixed by optimization.

Critical 187,665 URLs
75% of site

The data is Romandie-only, but the site ships four languages

Atlas covers French-speaking Switzerland exclusively. Checking the German sitemap for major German- and Italian-speaking cities returns nothing at all:

Zürich 0 · Bern 0 · Basel 0 · Luzern 0 · St. Gallen 0 · Winterthur 0 · Zug 0 · Aarau 0 · Chur 0 · Schaffhausen 0 · Lugano 0
Actual top cities: Genf 1,551 · Lausanne 1,002 · Aigle 321 · Freiburg 283 · Neuenburg 267

Magic Heidi's market is 70% German / 20% French / 5% Italian / 5% English. Atlas has no inventory at all in the 70% market.

Worse, the DE, IT and EN folders are real translations of Romandie businesses. /de/genf/… is a German page about a Geneva business. Nobody in Zürich searches for a Geneva locksmith in German; nobody in Geneva searches in German at all.

The majority-language facet duplicates its own parent

A first pass of this audit put this at 18,136 duplicates. That was wrong, and the corrected figure is smaller and more specific. The 4,534 depth-3 non-prestation URLs per locale are actually 2,806 language facets plus 1,728 tag facets, and most of the language facets are already handled correctly. Fetched live:

URLListingsRobots
/fr/lausanne/menuisier/24indexable
/fr/lausanne/menuisier/francophone/24 — same as parentindexable ← the bug
/fr/lausanne/menuisier/anglophone/2already noindex,follow
/fr/lausanne/menuisier/germanophone/2already noindex,follow

An existing MIN_TAG_INDEXABLE = 5 rule already noindexes small facets, so the minority-language pages are fine — and they're a real product feature, since "English-speaking carpenters in Lausanne" is a genuine filter. The defect is narrower: the majority-language facet returns the same set as its parent yet stays indexable, because the rule is purely count-based. That's 1,639 francophone URLs in the French folder.

The 1,728 tag facets must be left alone either way — they carry real traffic (decorateur-interieur 161 impressions, cleaning-service 105, peintre 52).

Medium 979 slugs
17,377 in DB corrected

The tag vocabulary is unvalidated LLM output

A first pass called this "two competing URL patterns." That framing was wrong. city/trade/ is category-scoped and city/prestation/tag/ is tag-scoped — a legitimate distinction, and the categories are a clean curated list of 74. The real defect is that the tag vocabulary is free-form model output that was never validated: the enrichment prompt asked for "a few free-form tags" without specifying language, casing, or character set, and the only write-time check is a type test and a truncation to 10. The database holds 17,377 distinct tags.

So English and French slugs for the same trade both persist, both go live, and both get indexed:

/fr/lausanne/prestation/painting/ → "Les 50 meilleures entreprises de peinture à Lausanne" /fr/lausanne/prestation/peinture/ → "Les 10 entreprises de peinture à Lausanne"

The pairs run deep across the French folder: painting 69 URLs vs peinture 43 · cleaning 62 vs nettoyage 36 · carpentry 19 vs menuiserie 29 · catering 24 vs traiteur 3 · accounting 35 vs comptabilite 4. Hairdressing alone is split across eight slugs (hair-salon, hairdresser, haircuts, haircut, coiffure, barber, and two spellings of salon de coiffure). GSC shows the cost — /fr/…/prestation/property-management/ sits at position 85.3, /fr/lausanne/prestation/heating/ at 77.3.

Medium 2,395 tags with
spaces in the slug

Slugs were never sanitised

Tags are written straight into the URL with no slugify and no percent-encoding — the URL builder interpolates the raw database value. Auditing the tag vocabulary turns up 2,395 tags containing spaces (graphic design, sole proprietorship, real estate), 619 containing non-ASCII (rénovation, comptabilité, auto-école), 26 with uppercase, three containing / (24/7 service — which breaks the route's segment count outright), three containing & (emitted unescaped into sitemap XML), and one empty string.

Place names leak in as services too — genève (46 URLs), neuchâtel (47), suisse (23), wallis (19), delémont (10) — producing the self-referential /fr/neuchatel/prestation/neuchâtel/. A denylist exists but compares ASCII slugs exactly, so the accented spellings slip straight past it. Review-sentiment adjectives also became landing pages: friendly-service (28), family-run (12), affordable, luxury.

Medium no inbound
links at all fixed
21 Aug

Atlas was an orphan subdomain

atlas.magicheidi.ch was not linked from magicheidi.ch anywhere — no mention on the homepage or /de/, absent from llms.txt, absent from the main sitemap index. As a subdomain it inherits no authority from the main domain, and it was receiving none through links either. A quarter-million pages hung off nothing.

Fixed 21 Aug 2026: a followed link to the directory now ships in the magicheidi.ch footer across all eight locales.

05

What to do

The strategic question comes first: should Atlas exist in this shape at all? It currently returns 94 clicks per 28 days, none commercial, against a build of 250,220 pages. That can't be fixed by optimization — the market, the language mix, and the SERP opposition are all wrong at once.

Option A — Cut to the defensible core

Recommended

Keep only what has a plausible path to ranking. Takes Atlas to roughly 3% of its current size. Steps 2, 4 and 5 are restated below from the corrected findings — an earlier draft of this plan overstated 2 and mis-framed 4.

  1. Serve only /fr/ (retiring 187,665 URLs). Return 410 for /de/, /it/ and /en/, redirect / to /fr/, and suppress the hreflang cluster while one locale is active. Implement it as a reversible ACTIVE_LANGS flag, not a deletion — German-speaking cantons are on the product roadmap, and re-enabling should be a one-line change.
  2. 410 the majority-language facet only (~1,639 francophone URLs), where the result set equals the parent's. Leave anglophone / germanophone / italophone alone — they're genuine filters and already noindexed — and leave all 1,728 tag facets alone, since they carry real traffic.
  3. Noindex every business detail page (~46,626). Keep them live and linked with noindex,follow so category pages still have somewhere to point, but stop asking Google to index 90-word pages that only win brand queries at 0% CTR. Drop the business sitemaps.
  4. Fix slug hygiene first — it's cheaper than step 5 and worth more. Slugify the tag segment in the URL builder (spaces, accents, uppercase, / and & can never reach a URL), normalise before the place-name denylist so accented city names are caught, and add XML escaping to the sitemap writer.
  5. Merge the EN/FR tag twins with a small alias map plus 301s — painting→peinture, cleaning→nettoyage, the hairdressing cluster→coiffure. Don't hand-curate all 979 slugs; gate indexability on an allowlist of genuine French service tags and noindex the rest.

Implemented 21 Aug 2026 on branch seo/option-a-prune, verified locally with 22/22 acceptance checks passing. Actual outcome: the indexable surface goes from 250,220 URLs to 15,305 — a 94% cut. That is larger than the ~7,000 this plan originally projected, because step 6 deliberately adds 3,117 canton-scoped tag pages that were previously sitemap-orphaned. The single French sitemap now carries 15,305 URLs with zero non-ASCII, zero spaces, zero duplicates and zero hreflang entries.

The deeper fix behind steps 4 and 5 is upstream and still open: the enrichment prompt asks for free-form tags and nothing validates them on write, so the vocabulary will re-pollute as new cantons are seeded unless that changes.

Option B — Fix the market mismatch first

If Atlas is strategically important, the data problem outranks the SEO problem: acquire German-speaking Switzerland inventory — Zürich, Bern, Basel, Luzern, St. Gallen, Winterthur, Zug — before touching anything else. That's where 70% of the market is. Run Option A's steps 2–5 regardless, but rebuild /de/ against real German-region businesses rather than deleting it. This is the larger investment, and I wouldn't start it until the local-pack problem has an answer.

Option C — Accept the SERP isn't winnable and repurpose

Every target SERP has a local pack fed by the same Google Maps data Atlas republishes, so directory pages that mirror it have no differentiated value. If Atlas stays, it needs something Google Maps doesn't have — Magic Heidi's own angle: which businesses are sole proprietorships, which are VAT-registered, legal form, UID data, industry benchmarks. That turns Atlas from another directory into a data asset that also feeds the accounting-software funnel, where the revenue intent actually lives.

Regardless of path

  1. Link Atlas from magicheidi.ch — done 21 Aug 2026. A followed footer link to the directory now ships across all eight locales. Worth also adding it to llms.txt and the main sitemap index. Note the ordering caveat: this passes equity into the current thin-page set until the pruning above lands, so the two should ship close together.
  2. Add canton-level tag pages to the sitemap. /{lang}/{canton}/prestation/{tag}/ pages render and are internally linked, but appear in zero sitemaps — the generator emits city-scoped entries only. They are the strongest category pages on the site: canton scope means more listings and less thinness, and 36 of them picked up impressions while sitemap-orphaned. /fr/canton-de-neuchatel/prestation/isolation/ drew 168 impressions — the highest of any category page on the site. This is the one place to add URLs rather than remove them.
  3. Re-measure on 15 Sep 2026, four weeks after any pruning ships. The metrics that matter are impressions per indexed page and category-page average position — not raw impressions.
  4. Renew SE Ranking or replace it. The Data API no longer works on prepaid credits; the whole account returns License expired. Every /seranking-* command and the 1 Sep re-measure are blocked until then.

What to expect after the prune

Search Console will look alarming for a few weeks, and that is the plan working rather than failing. Expect all of the following:

  • A large spike in "Not found (404)" and excluded URLs. Roughly 190,000 URLs start returning 410. Google will take weeks to recrawl them all, and Coverage will show the backlog the whole time.
  • Indexed-page count falls hard — that is the objective. The number to watch instead is impressions per indexed page.
  • Total impressions drop by roughly the 6,894 the DE/IT/EN folders were contributing. Those produced 42 clicks, all navigational.
  • Business-page impressions decay slowly after the noindex, since Google has to recrawl each one to see the tag.

Resubmit sitemap.xml in Search Console immediately after deploying — it is the fastest way to signal the new, much smaller URL set. Then hold until 15 Sep before judging anything; four weeks is the minimum for recrawl to settle.
